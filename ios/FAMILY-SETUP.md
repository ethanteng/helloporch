# Bedside and family beta

One universal Porch app supports iPad bedside mode and iPhone/iPad family mode. iPhones start in family mode; iPads keep the bedside clock. Change modes from caregiver settings, the family screen's **photo → Use this iPhone as the bedside Porch**, or (on a device that isn't set up as a bedside yet) **Use this device as a family member** on the bedside setup screen. Each mode keeps its own device pairing, so switching never removes your family pairing.

## Pair your family

1. Install the same updated TestFlight build on Mom's iPad and family members' iPhones.
2. On the iPad, tap the **gear** (a half-second hold works too). On first caregiver entry, choose and confirm your own six-digit PIN. An already-paired iPad needs no preset code. An older installation can enroll using its saved credentials. A brand-new bedside device uses a private activation link as described below. The initial display name is **Your loved one**; tap it at the top of caregiver mode to change it.
3. In **People**, tap an empty key, keep **Person** selected and choose **Invite them** (or **Choose from Family on Porch** for someone already on Porch). Select audio and/or video drop-in if appropriate. Both drop-in permissions start off. Porch asks for the corresponding iPad media permissions during setup. Their page then sets **Calls start as → Voice / Video**, their button color and their phone. You can also invite someone from **People → Invite someone** without putting them on the keypad.
4. Share the private invitation using the system share sheet. It's a link such as `https://helloporch.store/join#code=…`.
   - **With Porch installed,** tapping it on their iPhone opens Porch and joins.
   - **Without Porch,** the link opens a page that shows how to get Porch through TestFlight, then **Open invitation in Porch**, or **Copy invitation** to paste into **Invitation → Join your family**.

   Invitations expire after 24 hours and work for one device. Invite each additional device separately.
   - The code stays after the `#`, so it never reaches the website's logs, and the page never loads Google Analytics.
   - An invitation for a server other than the one the app is built with is still a `porch://pair` link.
5. On the iPad, turn on **Allow approved drop-ins**. Return to the clock. The family member can now make audio/video calls or use the drop-in types you approved.
6. After sharing, tap **Done**, then **Save** the person. The name you typed moves to the invitation and the **Name** field is left empty, so the key follows the name the family member chooses for themselves (see [Names and photos](#names-and-photos)); type a name there to always use yours instead. The editor shows **Waiting to accept invite**. Acceptance links that exact invitation to the saved person automatically; there is no additional linking step. Expired/cancelled invitations can be sent again. For a device already in your Porch, use **Choose from your Porch**. Optionally add a fallback phone number. Contacts imports and typed numbers are normalized to E.164 (for example, `(415) 867-6937` in the US becomes `+14158676937`). National numbers use the device's region; include `+country code` for overseas contacts. Extensions aren't supported.

Paired calls use the bundled server address and private device credentials. Family members never enter a server address or shared calling code; only family members approved for caregiver mode enter the caregiver PIN. Contacts have one combined details section with their name, Contacts import, audio/video preference and paired app. The app no longer offers calling-method, room, advanced-connection or daily-use settings. The browser prototype remains available for legacy manual-room testing.

## Caregiver mode from a family phone

The caregiver can approve family members to manage access from their own phone. Turn on **Caregiver mode** when creating their invitation, or later in **Caregivers & PIN** or on their page. An approved family member taps their photo, then **Caregiver mode**, and enters the household's six-digit caregiver PIN. Tap **Done** to return. Caregiver mode on a phone lists the keypad, display and PIN as set on the bedside iPad.

From a phone, caregiver mode covers the settings the server holds: the display name, each family member's name and photo, drop-in on/off, each device's audio/video drop-in, whether its calls drop in, and caregiver access, phone numbers approved for audio drop-in, invitations, removing devices, and [call history](#call-history). Keypad numbers and contacts (including a keypad contact's own name and photo), call preferences, the bedside display and the caregiver PIN can only be changed on the bedside iPad, which keeps its own copy of the PIN to renew phone-fallback approvals. Drop-ins enabled from a phone connect only if the bedside iPad already allows microphone and camera access.

The PIN is kept in the phone's memory only. Caregiver mode locks after ten idle minutes, when Porch leaves the foreground, or when the PIN or approval changes. Wrong PINs from a phone count against that phone alone (five per 15 minutes), so they can't lock out the bedside iPad. A caregiver can't remove their own phone or change their own caregiver access; another caregiver or the bedside iPad can.

To move a phone to a different family, tap your photo → **Join a different family** and use the new family's invitation. The phone stays in its current family until the new invitation is accepted, then leaves the old one automatically.

## Names and photos

Each family member chooses how they appear on the bedside: on their phone, tap their **photo** at the top right of the Porch screen, then add a photo (take one or choose from the library) and type their name. Leaving the name blank uses the name on their invitation. The bedside shows that name and photo on its keypad cards, incoming calls and drop-in announcements, the active call screen, and message playback, including for people without a keypad number. Photos are resized on the phone (at most 768 pixels, under 1 MB) and stored in the private Blob store with messages; the bedside downloads each one once and checks it matches what was uploaded. A photo uploaded but not saved within 20 minutes is deleted.

A caregiver can choose a different name or photo for anyone, which then shows instead of theirs. Each family member has one name and photo, used on their key, their calls and their messages:

- **A person's page** (tap them in **People**, on the bedside iPad or a caregiver's phone) sets them everywhere. Leave the name blank and save, or tap **Use their own photo**, to go back to theirs. The family member's Settings screen says when a caregiver's choice is showing.
- Someone on the keypad only by phone, without Porch, keeps the name and photo saved with their key.
- A keypad key saved before this update keeps the name or photo the caregiver gave it until that person's name or photo is next saved on their page, which moves it to their Porch profile.

So the bedside shows a family member's name and photo chosen by a caregiver, then their own, then (for the name) the invitation's. Removing someone's access deletes their photos.

## Messages and voicemail

Family members can send an audio or video message (up to two minutes) from **Send a message** on their Porch screen. When a call isn't answered, or the bedside Porch isn't available, the family app offers **Leave a message**: it beeps and records audio, like voicemail. On the bedside iPad, **Calls → Ring before voicemail** sets how long a call rings first, from 15 seconds to 2 minutes (45 seconds by default). The same wait applies to regular phone callers. Approved drop-ins always get 45 seconds for their announcement and countdown. Family phones need the updated app to follow the setting; older builds keep ringing for 45 seconds. Messages don't need the bedside iPad to be open; they wait until played.

On the bedside clock, a **Play 2 messages** notice appears at the top left once messages have downloaded. Tapping it plays them oldest first: a chime, then "Message from Ethan. Sent today at 2:14 PM" in the bedside language and voice, then the message. For a video message, a banner at the top ("Message from Ethan · Sent Today at 2:14 PM") stays on screen while the video plays. Playback continues until none are left. **Stop**, the keypad's messages key or the hang-up key stops it, and an incoming call or drop-in takes over. A message stopped partway stays in the queue; one played to the end is removed and won't play again. To give the messages notice a key, edit any keypad number in caregiver mode and choose **Messages**.

**Playing when someone sits down.** On the bedside iPad, **Messages → Play when someone is in front of the iPad** (off until a caregiver turns it on) starts waiting messages by themselves. While downloaded messages are waiting and the clock is showing, the front camera looks for a face close to the iPad for about two seconds. Someone across the room or walking past doesn't count. Then the screen glows, a chime plays, and the bedside voice says "You have 2 new messages," and playback starts. **Not now**, the messages key or the hang-up key waits instead; a person's key calls them. After Not now or Stop, it waits 30 minutes before trying again, unless a new message arrives. It only runs during the hours set there (8 AM until 9 PM to start; a start after the end runs overnight). It also stays off during calls, with the clock covered, when the iPad is hot, and when nothing is waiting. Detection uses Apple's on-device face detection on a few frames a second. Nothing is recorded, saved or sent, and the iPad's camera indicator shows while it watches. If [Check with the camera](#someone-at-the-ipad) is also on, a face it sees tells family someone is there. It needs camera access, which turning it on asks for. Remote caregiver mode on a phone doesn't show this setting, because it lives on the iPad.

Each family phone has one **Messages with Mom** thread, oldest at the top like a text conversation. Mom's messages are on the left in white, the phone's own on the right in green, each under a day heading with who sent it and the time. Under every message a colored tag says whether it's been played: green **Played** with the time, amber **Not played yet** (with whether it's still waiting to download or already on Mom's Porch), or gray once it's gone (never played within a week, removed by a caregiver, or unsent). Senders see only their own messages to Mom, not the rest of the family's. They can unsend one before it plays, and tap their own to play it back for as long as the recording is kept: while it waits, and for 90 days after it's played. Video messages show a preview frame, kept on the phone after the first view. **Record a message for Mom** at the bottom opens the same recorder as **Send a message**.

In **Messages**, caregivers (on the iPad, or from an approved phone with the PIN) can turn messages off, and preview or remove any waiting message. Removing a family member's access also removes their unplayed messages.

People calling the Porch phone number from a regular phone can leave voicemail too (setup: [incoming phone calls](INBOUND-PHONE-CALLS.md#voicemail)). If the caller's number is saved on a bedside keypad contact and the phone company verified the caller ID, it plays like any other message, announced with that contact's name. Caller ID can be faked, so unverified calls always wait for a caregiver. Otherwise it waits for a caregiver: approved caregivers see "phone voicemail needs your review" on their Porch screen, and **Let it play on Porch** or **Remove** in the waiting list.

### Messages from the bedside

When a Porch call from the bedside isn't answered, is declined, or can't reach the family member's Porch app, the bedside records a message for them, like voicemail. The call screen shows "Ethan didn't answer.", then Porch says "Ethan didn't answer. Please leave a message after the beep." in the bedside language and voice, beeps, and records: audio for a voice call, or video from the front camera for a video call, up to two minutes. **Send message**, the hang-up key, or reaching two minutes sends it. **Don't send**, or the hang-up key before the beep, sends nothing, and a recording with no sound in it isn't sent either. Porch then says "Your message is on its way to Ethan." and returns to the clock. If Ethan calls back while the message is being recorded, his call takes over and what was already said is sent.

Calls that ring through to a family member's regular phone (phone fallback) never record a Porch message: that phone's own voicemail can answer instead, and if it doesn't, the call simply ends. Only paired family members can get Porch messages; contacts with just a phone number use their phone's voicemail.

Messages leave the iPad in the background, so the bedside goes straight back to the clock. If the connection drops, the iPad keeps the recording and keeps trying for two days, including after Porch restarts. Messages stop retrying if the person's access is removed.

On the family phone, a **New message from Mom** button appears on the Porch screen and the app icon shows how many haven't been played. They show up in **Messages with Mom** on the left with a **New** tag and a green outline, and the thread opens at the first one that hasn't been played. Tapping one plays it and marks it played; it can be played again for 90 days after it was left, played or not. Each family member sees only the messages left for them. Updated family apps ask once to allow notifications and then show "Mom left you a message" with its type and length. Notifications use the same APNs key and app ID as incoming calls ([incoming-call setup](INCOMING-CALLS.md)). Without them, messages still appear when Porch is opened.

On the bedside iPad, turn off **Calls → Leave a message if no one answers**. It's on by default and stays on that iPad. The **Messages → Allow family messages** switch controls messages sent *to* the bedside only. Removing a family member's access, or their phone leaving the family, deletes the messages the bedside left for them.

## How calls behave

- Keep the bedside iPad open, unlocked and ready. With [APNs and Apple signing configured](INCOMING-CALLS.md), ordinary calls to paired family iPhones ring through CallKit while Porch is backgrounded, suspended, or the phone is locked. Open Porch once after installing/updating and allow microphone access to register. The family home screen reports whether background delivery is configured. Caregiver and pairing screens never auto-answer. Incoming family audio can continue behind the lock screen; outgoing and bedside/drop-in calls still need the app open.
- Ordinary incoming calls on family devices use the system **Answer** and **Decline** controls. Answering starts audio. For a video call, the camera turns on once Porch is open: right away if you answer while using Porch, or when you unlock and open Porch after answering from the lock screen. It never runs behind the lock screen. Locking or leaving Porch turns the camera off while audio continues; returning turns it back on unless you turned it off during the call. To start video calls, including ones you place, with the camera off, tap your photo and turn off **Start video calls with my camera on** (on by default) and tap **Camera on** when you want it. The setting stays on that phone. Bedside incoming calls ring repeatedly and show **Answer** and **Decline**. Answering a video call turns the bedside camera on; that phone setting doesn't apply to the bedside. Drop-ins play a chime and speak the caller's name and audio/video mode, then start the five-second countdown with a **Don't connect** button before answering automatically. If playback or speech fails, the call waits for manual acceptance instead of silently auto-answering. An audio drop-in never enables the camera. A short chime confirms connection or turning video on.
- Incoming names and photos follow [Names and photos](#names-and-photos), including spoken announcements and the active call screen. Renaming Ethan to David on his keypad contact takes effect without a new invite. Matching uses the paired device/invitation ID, so two people with the same name don't become linked. An approved caller without a slot uses the name and photo a caregiver chose, or their own, or the invitation's name.
- Drop-in needs both the master switch and that device's specific audio/video approval. Pause drop-ins, change permissions, cancel unused invitations or remove a family device in **Drop-in**, **People** and each person's page. Removing access ends its current call and requires a new invitation to reconnect.
- For a trusted family member, turn on **their page → [Name]'s calls drop in too**. Their **Audio call** and **Video call** then drop in too, with the same announcement and countdown, but only as the drop-in types they're approved for: with audio drop-in alone, their video calls still ring. This needs the master switch on and Porch open on the bedside iPad; otherwise their calls ring as usual (by push if Porch is closed). **Drop-in** lists them with **Calls drop in**.
- People without the app can drop in with audio by calling the household's Porch phone number, if the caregiver approves their number under **Drop-in → Add a phone number**, or with **Calls from their number drop in** on a keypad person's page. These calls get the same announcement and countdown. They drop in only while the master switch is on, the bedside clock is open, and their phone company verifies the caller ID. Other calls ring for a normal answer. See [inbound telephone calls](INBOUND-PHONE-CALLS.md#audio-drop-in-by-phone).
- To stop strangers and robocalls, turn on **Calls → Block unknown callers**. Then only keypad contacts' numbers, approved phone drop-ins, and numbers you allow can ring. Other callers hear their phone company's message that the call can't be completed, can't leave voicemail, and their numbers wait in **Blocked numbers** until you tap **Allow**. See [blocking unknown callers](INBOUND-PHONE-CALLS.md#blocking-unknown-callers).
- Mom's outgoing calls try Porch first. An unavailable family app or an unanswered call can fall back to its configured regular phone number. The wait is set in **Calls → Try their phone after**. Porch's answer and the phone handoff are coordinated atomically so an answered Porch call doesn't also dial the phone. As soon as a person's key is pressed, the bedside chimes and says who it's calling ("Calling David.") in the bedside language and announcement voice; the natural voice is used once its clip has been prepared, otherwise the built-in one. Ringback plays during both dialing stages, starting once that sentence is said; the iPad also waits for it before joining the call's audio, which would otherwise cut the sentence off. Hanging up while it speaks stops it.
- Only one Porch call can be active per household. Foreground presence and call state are checked every few seconds; family background ringing uses a VoIP push followed by authenticated call-state checks. Decline, hangup, expiry or lost-device heartbeats release the call. Calls are limited to 30 minutes, or an hour from when a phone number is added (below); phone fallback retains its shorter server limits.

## Someone at the iPad

Under the bedside's name, family phones show **Someone is at the iPad** when someone was there in the last 90 seconds, and otherwise how long ago someone was: "Someone was at the iPad 25 minutes ago". It helps them decide whether to call now. It never says no one is there, because Porch can miss someone lying in bed or across the room who can still hear a call ring. After a week it shows nothing, and it's hidden while the phone can't reach Porch.

- **Turning it off.** **Privacy → Show family when someone is at the iPad** is on to start. A caregiver can turn it off on the bedside iPad or in caregiver mode on a phone. Off, family see nothing about it, the server deletes the last time, and the iPad stops reporting and forgets anything it noticed meanwhile.
- **Keys and touches.** While it's on, any touch on the bedside screen, any key on a keyboard or number pad, and any key or hang-up on a Cell2Jack phone count, in every Porch screen, including caregiver mode.
- **Check with the camera.** On the bedside iPad, **Privacy → Check with the camera** (off until a caregiver turns it on, and it asks for camera access) also counts a face close to the iPad, while sharing is on. About every 30 seconds while the clock is showing, the front camera looks for up to five seconds, and stops as soon as a face has stayed for a second. It doesn't look during calls, with the clock covered, or when the iPad is hot. Faces seen while it watches to [play messages](#messages-and-voicemail) count too. The camera indicator shows during each look. It uses the same on-device face detection as playing messages: no image is recorded, saved or sent. Remote caregiver mode on a phone doesn't show this setting, because it lives on the iPad.
- **What family learn.** Only when someone was last noticed, never how or who. An aide or a visitor counts as someone. The iPad reports how many seconds ago with each check-in, so its clock doesn't matter, and the server keeps only the latest time.

## Call history

Caregivers can see the calls to and from the bedside iPad for the last 90 days (up to 100 calls), newest first and grouped by day: on the iPad in **Caregiver mode → Call history**, or on an approved caregiver's phone in **Caregiver mode → Calls → Call history**. Tap the refresh button to update it. Each call shows who it was with, whether it came in or went out, audio or video (or a drop-in), when it started, and how it went:

- **Talked 12 min**: answered; the time counts the bedside's part, so it stops when Mom hangs up even if the call went on without her.
- **Missed** (in red): a call to the bedside that nobody answered. **No answer**: Mom's call that nobody answered. Mom's unanswered Porch calls also say whether the family phone showed the call (**Their phone showed the call**) or never got it (**Didn't reach their phone**); the second points to background ringing not being set up on that phone, or the phone being offline.
- **Declined**, **Couldn't reach their Porch** (Porch couldn't reach their app at all), **Ringing now** and **On the call now**.
- **Called their phone**: the bedside rang someone's regular phone, by itself or as phone fallback. Porch can't tell whether a regular phone was answered.

Phone callers are named by their approved drop-in name or, on the iPad, the keypad contact saved with that number, and their number is shown too; caller ID can be faked. Names are kept as Porch showed them at the time, including for someone later removed. The current call is listed as soon as it starts. History begins with the server update that added it; earlier calls weren't kept.

## Adding family to a call

While you're on a Porch call with Mom, you can ring another family member into it, such as your brother. Tap **Add someone** on the call screen and choose them. Their phone rings like any Porch call, named "Ethan with Mom", and answering joins them to the call with audio or video, matching the call. Everyone with their camera on shares the screen: side by side on a wide screen, stacked on a tall one. Without video, a tile shows their photo (on the bedside) or name. The bedside says "Calling David…", then "David joined the call".

- Anyone in the family who is on the call with Mom can add someone, once you're both connected. The list shows the other family members paired with this Porch whose app can ring now. Someone who was rung in can't add anyone else.
- One person rings at a time, for the standard ring time, up to five tries per call. If they decline or don't answer, the screen says so and you can try again or choose someone else. If their Porch can't be reached, you're told right away.
- The call goes on while two people are still on it. If you hang up, Mom and your brother keep talking. If Mom hangs up, you and your brother keep talking, and she can't rejoin. A phone counts only while the caregiver who added it is still on, so the bedside is never left alone with a phone, and a phone you added hangs up when you leave. A device that stops checking in for about 35 seconds drops off the same way. When fewer than two people would remain, hanging up ends the call for everyone.
- Removing a rung-in family member's access cancels their invitation if it's still ringing, or takes them off the call, which goes on if two people remain. Removing access from the family member who started the call with Mom ends it.

## Adding a phone number to a call

A caregiver can bring a regular phone into their call with the bedside, for example a bank that needs Mom on the line. Call Mom on Porch (or answer her call), then tap **Add someone** on the call screen and choose **A phone number…** (if nobody else in the family can be rung in, the button reads **Add a phone** and opens the form directly). Enter who you're calling, such as **Bank**, and their number, or **Choose from Contacts**, then **Call and add to this call**. Everyone hears it ring. The bedside shows "Calling Bank…", then "Bank joined the call". Mom's screen never shows the number.

- Only family phones that a caregiver approved can add a phone number, and only once both of you are connected. Other family members and the bedside can't dial numbers. It needs the same outbound trunk as phone fallback (`LIVEKIT_SIP_OUTBOUND_TRUNK_ID`).
- US and Canadian numbers work, including toll-free ones. Caribbean and Bermuda numbers in the +1 area, premium-rate 900 and 976 numbers, and numbers outside +1 are refused. Twilio's geographic permissions on the trunk remain the backstop.
- One phone at a time, up to five per call. **Keypad** sends key presses for its menus ("press 1 for…"). **Hang up Bank** ends just that phone. If the bank hangs up or doesn't answer, the screen says so and you can add another number.
- The phone belongs to the caregiver who added it. If they hang up, or their phone goes offline, the phone hangs up too, and the call ends unless two family members are still on it, so Mom is never left alone with a caller she didn't choose. If Mom presses her hang-up key while the caregiver is still on, only Mom leaves; the caregiver keeps talking to the bank and ends the call when done. Mom can't rejoin that call, and her Porch shows as busy until it ends.
- Keep Porch open on your phone during a call you placed to Mom: like other outgoing Porch calls, leaving Porch or locking the phone ends it, phone number included. A call Mom placed to you, answered with **Answer**, keeps going behind the lock screen.
- The bank sees the household's own Porch number as caller ID, so calling it back rings the bedside. A household without a Porch number shows the outbound trunk's number. See [a number for each household](INBOUND-PHONE-CALLS.md#a-number-for-each-household).
- A silent hold doesn't trip the bedside's **Disconnect after silence** while a phone is on the call. A call with a phone number lasts up to an hour from when the number was dialed, and the phone itself is cut off after an hour.

## Keypad actions

There are twelve stable slots: **1–9, 0**, then **\*** and **#**. A new keypad starts with **9** toggling video and **0** answering or hanging up; every other key has no action until you assign it. Existing six-person setups keep the people already saved, and their empty keys show as **No action**. Edit any slot to choose **No action**, **Person**, **Call control**, **Video toggle**, or **Messages**. Any key can call a person, be a control, or be set back to **No action**. A keyboard's * and # count wherever its layout types them, usually with Shift. Touch controls remain available even if no keypad controls are assigned.

For the call-control key: tap and release to answer an ordinary incoming call; hold one second to decline it. Tap to stop a drop-in during its announcement, or to hang up an active/dialing call, including phone fallback. The video key toggles your camera during a video call; voice-only and regular phone calls keep it off. Holding a digit does not repeatedly trigger it. Keys work while Porch is foregrounded; caregiver text fields continue to accept typing.

## Typing in caregiver settings

The native iPad/iPhone keyboard is the default. Connecting or disconnecting a USB/Bluetooth keypad doesn't change that choice. **Porch keyboard** opens the optional built-in touch keyboard. Switching keeps the text already entered.

iPadOS can hide its software keyboard when an external keypad is attached, and its system keyboard controls may also be absent. **Disconnect the keypad during setup to use the native keyboard**, or choose **Porch keyboard** to keep the keypad connected. With a keypad connected, the native-keyboard button is labeled **Keyboard help** and explains these choices. It does not promise to force the system keyboard open. Opening help from the Porch keyboard keeps that input method selected until you choose another option.

If the system Shortcuts bar is available, its keyboard button → **Show Keyboard** is another option, as described in [Apple's keyboard guide](https://support.apple.com/en-euro/guide/ipad/ipaddd28d7ed/27/ipados/27). Porch preserves the system assistant controls, but cannot guarantee that iPadOS displays them. In Guided Access, keep **Touch** and **Software Keyboards** enabled during setup; these controls are in **Session Settings** (or **Options**) when the session is paused. See [Apple's Guided Access instructions](https://support.apple.com/en-nz/111795).

## Display & voice

Open **Caregiver mode → Display & voice** and choose **Dark** or **Day & night**. **Both always show the current local date and time in large text.** Day & night adds the time-of-day and season in words, a sun or moon traveling across the sky, and a seasonal tree and landscape. The background brightens through the morning to a blue daytime sky, warms to peach and pink near sunset, and darkens to navy at night. The large clock uses dark lettering on a light wash by day and light lettering on a dark wash at night, keeping the current date and time readable throughout the cycle. The scene updates each minute with a short dissolve; Reduce Motion removes that dissolve. There is no perpetual decorative animation, location request, or external weather service.

Set **Day begins** and **Night begins** to match the loved one's routine (initially 6 AM and 6 PM). The scene illustrates that schedule, rather than calculating astronomical sunrise/sunset or moon phase. Seasons use calendar months and the chosen **Northern / Southern** hemisphere (initially northern). Keep the iPad's date, time, and time zone correct in system settings.

Choose a language under **Bedside language** and tap **Save** to translate the clock/date, day/night and seasons, contact picker, incoming calls and call controls.

| Option | Display language | Drop-in announcement voice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| English | English | English (`en-US`) |
| 繁體中文 | Traditional Chinese | Taiwan Mandarin (`zh-TW`) |
| 简体中文 | Simplified Chinese | Mainland Mandarin (`zh-CN`) |
| 廣東話 | Cantonese in Traditional characters | Cantonese (`zh-HK`) |
| Español | Spanish | Spanish (`es-ES`) |

English remains the default; caregiver settings and family mode stay in English. Names stay exactly as entered by the caregiver. Language changes preserve the daily schedule, hemisphere, contacts and pairing. The contact picker uses much larger names and keypad numbers, with wrapping text and no phone icon; scroll to reach additional people.

Under **Announcement voice** on the same screen, choose **System default** or a voice available on this iPad for the selected bedside language. English and Spanish include regional accents. Taiwan Mandarin, mainland Mandarin and Cantonese each show their own matching voices; a voice cannot be used across all five languages. On iPadOS 17 and later, novelty voices such as Bubbles or Zarvox are not listed. Voice names include the locale and, when available, Enhanced or Premium quality. Each language remembers its own choice on this device. **Preview announcement** plays the current selection without saving or placing a call; tap **Save** to keep it. Leaving without saving discards changes.

To add voices, download them in the iPad's Accessibility speech settings and return to Porch. Availability varies with iPadOS and downloaded voices. If a saved voice is removed or no longer matches the language, Porch uses that language's system default and shows a message in the picker. If no matching voice can play, drop-ins require manual acceptance.

In **Calls → Test sounds**, use **Ringing** and **Drop-in announcement** before relying on drop-in. These play only on this device and do not place a call. The screen reports the current audio output and media volume. Turn up media volume with the physical volume buttons and verify that sound reaches the intended iPad speaker or connected audio device. Alerts explicitly acquire a playback session shared with LiveKit; they do not depend on a one-shot system notification sound. A voice for the selected language must be available for spoken announcements. If speech cannot play, the call requires manual acceptance. Zero volume, connected headphones, or system interruptions can still make audio inaudible; check the actual bedside iPad.

## Caregiver PIN

The first caregiver entry prompts you to choose and confirm a PIN. Later entries ask for that PIN. Tap the gear or hold it for half a second. Change the PIN under **Caregivers & PIN → Change caregiver PIN**. Five incorrect guesses cause a 15-minute lockout for that household. Internet access is required to unlock settings.

The server stores only a salted PIN digest, using the server secret as a pepper. The original long `PORCH_CONTACT_SETUP_CODE` remains server-side for recovery and signing phone approvals. **Do not replace that environment variable with six digits.** Existing device phone approvals survive a PIN change. Other households have independent PINs and attempt limits.

## A completely new bedside iPad

A server operator creates a private, one-use activation link. This authorizes enrollment without shipping a shared secret inside the app or accepting anonymous registrations to the calling service.

```sh
node --env-file=.env.local scripts/create-bedside-invite.mjs
```

The command reads the server's long recovery secret privately and writes `.env.bedside-activation.local` with mode 0600. It never prints the activation URL or PIN. The link is `https://helloporch.store/setup#code=…` for Porch's own server. Open it on the new iPad: it opens Porch, or a page that shows how to get Porch first. You can also paste it into the first caregiver setup screen. Choose the bedside name, choose and confirm a PIN, and continue. Links work for one device, expire after 24 hours and support a retry on the same device after a lost response. Each additional household gets its own PIN. There is no generated/default caregiver PIN file in the new setup flow. Keep activation links private; family recipients use separate family invitations.

Existing paired devices choose a PIN once without a new activation link. After that, changing it requires the current PIN. Legacy shared PINs are accepted only as a compatibility fallback for households that haven't chosen their own PIN yet; a household-specific PIN supersedes that fallback. Keep the long server recovery/signing secret for operator recovery and existing phone approvals.

## Server and privacy

Keep the LiveKit/SIP settings and long signing/recovery secret from the root setup guide. For messages, create a **private** Vercel Blob store and connect it to the calling server project, which adds `BLOB_READ_WRITE_TOKEN`; then redeploy. Without it, sending a message reports that messages aren't set up. For incoming iPhone calls, configure the APNs signing key and app ID as described in [incoming-call setup](INCOMING-CALLS.md). Connect Upstash Redis and provide either `UPSTASH_REDIS_REST_URL`/`UPSTASH_REDIS_REST_TOKEN` or Vercel's `KV_REST_API_URL`/`KV_REST_API_TOKEN` aliases. The free database was provisioned with automatic paid upgrades disabled. Deploy the new server before using the new app; older manual-room builds remain compatible.

The default data namespace is `porch:family:v1:`. Local verification must set an isolated `PORCH_FAMILY_STORE_PREFIX=porch:family:preview:v1:`. Redis CAS operations coordinate shared state across server instances. Storage outages fail closed; there is no process-memory fallback. Use TLS and keep write credentials server-side.

Redis stores household/display names, hashed device/invitation secrets, permissions, presence, when someone was last noticed at the bedside iPad, the current call and hashed PIN/lockout state. It also keeps message records: each sender's history (type, length, sent/delivered/played times, and for the bedside's messages, which family member each is for), the bedside queue, and which bedside messages each family member hasn't played. It stores each family phone's APNs notification token next to its VoIP token. Message audio and video are stored in the private Blob store; devices only receive signed upload/download links that expire within an hour. Unplayed messages are deleted after seven days; played messages' media is deleted after 90 days, while the record stays. Messages the bedside leaves for family are kept for 90 days after they're left, played or not. Until a bedside message is sent, its recording stays on the iPad. The bedside iPad keeps downloaded messages until they're played or removed. Redis doesn't store full contact books or the names on keypad contacts. It does store the phone numbers and names a caregiver approves for phone drop-in, the phone numbers saved on keypad contacts (so blocking unknown callers can let them through), and the numbers a caregiver allowed or that were blocked, with their call times. Only the bedside and caregiver mode can see them. Local slots remain on the device. Keychain stores private device credentials, caregiver credentials and scoped phone approvals. LiveKit handles media/connection metadata; SIP fallback sends its destination to LiveKit and the telephone provider. When the server has an ElevenLabs key, the sentences the bedside speaks (which include family names) are sent to ElevenLabs to be read in the family's natural voice, and the clips are kept in the private Blob store and on the bedside iPad; Redis keeps each household's voice choice and a daily character count. Calls aren't recorded; only messages people choose to record are stored. Transport encryption is used; application-level end-to-end encryption isn't configured. Update App Store privacy disclosures for this data handling before distributing the beta.

This is device pairing, without email accounts or account recovery UI. Legacy shared-code rooms retain their older trust model. Public release still needs push delivery, recovery/deletion flows, abuse controls and distributed SIP dialing limits. Revocation deletes the active media room; issued media join tokens last up to one minute, so token replay/rejoin remains a prototype limitation.

## Verify

Run Node tests, type checking, the Next production build, PorchCore tests and UIKit tests on iPhone and iPad. Backend tests use fake media clients and never place telephone calls.

For local simulator verification only:

```sh
# .env.local must contain an isolated preview namespace and private server settings.
npm run dev -- --port 3028
node --env-file=.env.local scripts/seed-family-preview.mjs http://localhost:3028 /tmp/porch-family-preview
```

The seed creates fictional devices and private mode-0600 fixture files with the preview PIN `246810`. Copy the appropriate file into the simulator app's Documents directory as `PorchDebugFamily.json`, then launch Debug with `--family-fixture --family-silent`. Fixtures are consumed once. Silent mode suppresses microphone publication and ringback for automated verification. Build with `CODE_SIGNING_ALLOWED=YES CODE_SIGN_IDENTITY=-` for simulator Keychain testing; Xcode embeds the simulated entitlements. Unsigned build artifacts don't have Keychain access. Release builds contain no fixture loader or silent mode.

Physical-device acceptance: test all twelve physical keys, including * and #, and remapped controls; tap/hold answer/decline and hangup during dialing/phone fallback; toggle the video camera; confirm the current date/time stay visible in both bedside views, landscape/portrait and Reduce Motion; accept an invite and confirm the saved person links after relaunch; pair and relaunch both devices; approve audio only and confirm video is unavailable; enable video and test both directions; stop/pause/revoke during a drop-in; leave the iPad in caregiver mode or background and verify it doesn't auto-answer; test caller cancellation during the countdown; test permission denial, network loss and concurrent callers; verify real microphone/camera, speaker routing, Guided Access, and phone fallback with your authorized test number.

Language/audio acceptance: repeat with 繁體中文, 简体中文, 廣東話 and Español. Save, relaunch and verify date/time and contact cards remain readable in both orientations; check names are unchanged and all call controls use the selected language. Confirm the language choice fits on a small phone and that changing it preserves the daily schedule and hemisphere. Rename a linked person, relaunch, then confirm ordinary calls and audio/video drop-ins show and announce the new name, including after answering. Run both local sound tests at the bedside. Verify repeating incoming ringing, audio/video announcements in the selected language (including Cantonese for 廣東話) followed by a full five-second countdown, cancellation during speech/countdown, the connection/video-on chime, "Calling …" spoken before ringback on outgoing calls, and ringback during Porch and phone fallback. Simulator waveforms and injected-audio checks do not confirm physical speaker audibility.

Voice picker acceptance: for each of the five languages, check that only matching voices appear, preview an available choice, save and relaunch, then confirm **Calls → Test sounds → Drop-in announcement** and real audio/video drop-ins use that voice. Switch between languages and confirm each choice is remembered. Preview a different voice and leave without saving; the saved voice should remain. Confirm preview stops when switching language or leaving settings. Download a new matching voice in system settings, return, and confirm it appears. Remove a saved downloaded voice and verify the picker reports the fallback and announcements still use the correct language. Test on the bedside iPad's actual speakers, including Enhanced/Premium voices if installed.

Keyboard acceptance: with the keypad disconnected, tap name, phone and PIN fields and type with the native keyboard. Connect the physical keypad while editing; confirm native input remains selected even if iPadOS hides the software keyboard. Tap **Keyboard help** and verify its instructions, use **Porch keyboard** and type with the keypad connected, then disconnect the keypad and return to **iPad keyboard**. Confirm text and focus are preserved. Repeat with Guided Access enabled and on a family iPhone. Simulator input/focus checks do not prove physical keypad/software-keyboard compatibility.
